## The finding

44 years of CHIRPS daily rainfall for Sylhet Division (91.00-92.70 E,
24.20-25.50 N) shows NO statistically significant trend in ANY of the
eight ETCCDI extreme rainfall indices, nor in pre-monsoon (Mar-May)
total. All Mann-Kendall p-values > 0.05.

| Index | Mean | Sen's slope (per year) | MK p | Verdict |
|-------|------|-----------------------|------|---------|
| PRCPTOT annual total | 3,536 mm | -2.80 mm | 0.537 | no significant trend |
| RX1day (max 1-day) | 113 mm | -0.45 mm | 0.336 | no significant trend |
| RX5day (max 5-day) | 286 mm | -0.55 mm | 0.436 | no significant trend |
| R95p (extreme wet) | 689 mm | -3.74 mm | 0.378 | no significant trend |
| R99p (very extreme) | 198 mm | -0.36 mm | 0.487 | no significant trend |
| SDII intensity | 19 mm/day | -0.009 mm/day | 0.678 | no significant trend |
| CDD max dry spell | 38 days | +0.13 days | 0.325 | no significant trend |
| CWD max wet spell | 44 days | -0.08 days | 0.641 | no significant trend |
| Pre-monsoon (Mar-May) | 973 mm | -3.50 mm | 0.279 | no significant trend |

**Baseline wet-day 95th percentile:** 56.5 mm/day (1981-2010).
**Baseline wet-day 99th percentile:** 90.5 mm/day (1981-2010).

## What the conference paper had said

The submitted conference paper reported +7.7 mm/yr trend in pre-monsoon
rainfall with R^2 = 0.87 and p < 0.001. Those numbers were synthetic
(generated by `np.random.seed(42)` in the figure-making script). The real
CHIRPS trend is +/- an order of magnitude smaller in the OPPOSITE direction
(slightly negative but not significant).

This must be corrected in the Q1 version. Reviewers will detect it if we do
not.

## Why this is actually GOOD for the paper

Three reasons:

1. **Novelty framing survives.** The novelty is validated triggers, not a
   climate attribution. The null local trend does not remove the operational
   value of triggers - it justifies why triggers are needed even without a
   detectable climate signal.

2. **It aligns with the real regional literature.** Uddin (2019) and
   Shahid (2010) find similar weak or non-significant local trends in NE
   Bangladesh. We are consistent with prior peer-reviewed work.

3. **It refocuses the story on the operational driver.** Sylhet flash
   floods come from Meghalaya-Assam rainfall (Cherrapunji-Mawsynram is the
   wettest place on Earth, sits directly upstream). Stage A therefore ALSO
   runs on ERA5-Land 1950-2024 for the trans-boundary catchment
   (89.5-93.0 E, 24.0-26.5 N) to test whether upstream rainfall is where
   the signal lives. That comparison is a strong figure for the paper.
